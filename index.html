Selected work — 5 projects

01

Water Tank Volume Monitoring via IoT

Individual project · Internship at PT Pelindo Terminal Petikemas New Makassar · 2026

Designed during an 8th-semester internship to let staff check a water tank's fill level remotely instead of visiting the site in person. An SR04M-2 ultrasonic sensor measures water level, a Wemos D1 Mini calculates fill percentage, and the reading is pushed over MQTT to a real-time Ubidots dashboard. Currently a working prototype rather than a deployed solution, but it proves a low-cost way to cut unnecessary site visits.

What I learned — how to correctly identify a sensor's communication protocol (the SR04M-2 uses TRIG/ECHO pins, not UART as I first assumed), and how to diagnose unstable readings caused by loose jumper wires.

02

Temperature & Humidity Detection System

Group project (Kelompok 3) · Class assignment · 2024

A real-time temperature and humidity system built around a DHT11 sensor and an ESP32, with readings displayed on a Blynk dashboard over Wi-Fi. My role was hardware assembly and wiring. We tested it across three conditions — a non-AC room (30.20°C), an AC room (27.60°C), and outdoors (28.50°C) — and confirmed it reliably told the environments apart.

What I learned — the practical side of sensor wiring, including why a pull-up resistor matters for stable signal readings.

03

3-Joint SCARA-Inspired Robotic Arm

Group project (Kelompok 3) · Class assignment · 2025

A 3D model and kinematic study of a SCARA-type arm — a design built for fast, precise pick-and-place work. My contribution was the parametric model in Fusion 360, covering all three joints: a 360° revolving base, a rotational elbow, and a linear vertical stroke. We simulated its motion to visualize the combined cylindrical work volume.

What I learned — how each joint type directly shapes a robot's reach and precision, and stronger parametric 3D modeling technique.

04

Custom PCB-Based LED Driver Circuit

Group project (Group 14, 8 members) · Lab assignment · 2023

An AC-to-DC power supply built from discrete components — bridge diode, zener diode, capacitors, resistors — hand-soldered onto a custom-etched PCB to safely drive nine LEDs. My focus was the PCB design and soldering. Calculated power draw was 7.29 W; measured power came in at 7.61 W, a close enough match to validate the design.

What I learned — how much precision matters when hand-soldering a custom board, and how to connect theoretical circuit calculations to a working physical prototype.

05

Automatic Clothesline Roof

Group research project (Kelompok 9) · Class assignment · 2025

A clothesline roof that closes itself when it senses rain or falling light, using a rain sensor and an LDR read by an ATmega8535, controlling a servo and buzzer. My role was program logic and debugging. Testing surfaced two real problems in sequence — an underpowered servo, then the rain and LDR sensors interfering with each other — solved by isolating one variable at a time.

What I learned — a structured, one-variable-at-a-time approach to hardware debugging.
